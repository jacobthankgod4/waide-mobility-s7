The map

From floor plan to searchable twin.

The map is built once, from the plans the venue already has, and updated when the building changes. Everything else runs on top of it.

Inputs

  • Floor plans in PDF, CAD or image format
  • Tenant or department list with names and units
  • Shelf and product data where product search is in scope
  • A walkthrough with someone who knows the building

What gets drawn

  • Every public level, including parking
  • Units, entrances, lifts, stairs and facilities
  • Walkable paths between destinations
  • Step-free connections, marked separately

What gets indexed

  • Store, department, counter and facility names
  • Product and category names, where supplied
  • Synonyms and local-language terms
  • Opening hours and temporary closures

If the plans are out of date, we survey the space first. That is a separate, quoted piece of work, and we say so before starting.

The route

How a search becomes a route.

  1. The visitor scans a QR code

    The code opens the venue in the phone browser. There is no app, no login and no location permission required to start.

  2. They search for a destination

    Product, store, department, facility or exit. The search index returns the closest match and shows where it sits on the map.

  3. The route is built on the path graph

    Routes follow walkable paths, change level at lifts or stairs, and avoid closures marked by the venue. Step-free routes use lifts and ramps only.

  4. The visit is recorded, without a profile

    The search, the route and whether it was completed are logged against the venue, not against a named person.

The dashboard

The six things a pilot measures.

These are the definitions fixed before a pilot starts, so the result cannot be chosen after the fact. The same six appear in the Novare Lekki measurement plan.

Metric Definition What it answers
Search completion Share of searches that end with the visitor reaching the destination Does the map get people there?
Drop-off zone The zone where abandoned routes cluster Where does the building lose people?
Top unmet searches Searches with no match or no completed route, by volume What do visitors want that the venue does not have, or cannot name?
QR scan to route Share of scans that start a route in the same session Is the entry point working?
Zone dwell change Time spent in a zone, compared with the same period before launch Did movement patterns change?
Route completion time Median time from route start to arrival, for the same destination type Are routes getting shorter or longer?

Reporting is weekly during a pilot. Baselines are captured before launch. No result is published without a sample size and the period it covers.

Data

What we collect, and what we do not.

Collected

  • Search terms entered in the venue
  • Routes started, completed or abandoned
  • Zone-level movement derived from routes
  • Device type, for compatibility testing

Not collected

  • Names, phone numbers or email addresses
  • Persistent profiles across visits
  • Precise location outside the venue
  • Payment or loyalty data

The venue owns the data collected in its building. Waide removes personal identifiers, does not sell personal data, and agrees retention terms in the deployment contract. Security and data-handling documentation is available during procurement.

Limits

What the platform does not do.

A shorter list than most vendors publish, and the reason the rest of this site can be specific.

Not offered today What that means
Hardware No beacons, kiosks or sensors are required, and none are included. If a venue wants them, they are scoped separately.
Security and CCTV integration Incident pinning, camera feeds and guard dispatch are not part of the product.
POS and ERP integration Reports are delivered from Waide and can be exported. Direct system integration is not included today.
Advertising The map does not serve ads or sell placements.
Accessibility certification Accessible routes are mapped and tested in-house. No third-party certification is claimed.

Questions

Common questions

What do you need to start mapping?

A current floor plan, a tenant or department list and a short walkthrough. If the plans are out of date, we survey the space first.

How are routes updated when a store moves?

The venue tells us, or the tenancy data changes, and the map is updated. Directory and tenant changes are included for the term of the agreement; a physical renovation is re-mapped as a separate job.

Does the visitor need to share their location?

No. The QR scan opens a map with a search box. Location permission is only relevant if a venue adds indoor positioning later.

Can you prove the routes are correct?

Routes are walked on site before launch. During a pilot, route completion and drop-off by zone show where the map is failing, and the map is corrected.

Where is the data stored?

In the region agreed in the contract. The venue can request an export of its own data at any time, and retention is set in the agreement.

Can we see the measurements before we commit?

Yes. The six metrics above are fixed in the pilot scope, with baselines and reporting dates, before any work starts.
