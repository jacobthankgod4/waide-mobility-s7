The term

What geoconquesting usually means.

Geoconquesting targets a message at people who are near a competitor's location. It is a marketing technique, not a wayfinding feature.

Targeting

A location boundary picks out phones near a competitor, sometimes with a time or behaviour filter.

Delivery

An ad network serves the message inside another app or website. The map is not involved.

Measurement

The ad platform reports impressions, clicks and sometimes store visits. Attribution is the hard part, and the numbers are often modelled.

None of this is part of Waide. We do not have an ad network, we do not buy location data, and we do not target people outside the building we mapped.

What we measure

Demand inside the building, not around it.

The useful signal is already inside the venue: what people search for, where they stop and where they give up. That is measured from the map, not from a third-party data broker.

Top unmet searches

What visitors look for and the venue does not have, or cannot name. This is a merchandising and leasing signal.

Drop-off zones

Where routes are abandoned. It shows which parts of the building are hard to reach, not which competitor is nearby.

Zone traffic

Movement by zone and by hour, derived from routes started in the venue. It is aggregate, not personal.

The six pilot metrics are defined on the platform page. Each result is published with a baseline and a sample size.

The line

Where we stop.

Inside the venue

  • Searches and routes started by visitors
  • Aggregate zone movement
  • Facility and service demand

Outside the venue

  • No precise location collection
  • No competitor targeting
  • No purchase of third-party movement data
  • No sale of venue data to advertisers

Questions

Common questions

Can you target shoppers near another mall?

No. We do not collect location outside the building and do not run advertising. A marketing agency with its own data would be the right conversation, not Waide.

Do you sell venue data to advertisers?

No. The venue owns the data collected in its building, and Waide does not sell it.

Can we see where visitors come from?

Not today. The map records what happens inside the venue. It does not know a visitor's home area or how they travelled.

Is this the same as proximity advertising?

No. Proximity advertising pushes an offer based on a person's position inside or near a place. Geoconquesting targets people near a competitor. We do neither.

What should we use instead?

Measure the demand you already have. Searches with no match, drop-off zones and zone traffic tell you what to stock, where to sign and which unit is hard to reach.

Work from the data you already own.

A pilot measures what visitors search for inside your building. That is enough to change a directory, a sign or a tenancy conversation.
