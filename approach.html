How we deliver

How we plan and deliver a mapping project.

We agree on what needs to be mapped, check the source information, test the results and explain what is ready at handover.

The stages are a working method, not a claim of formal certification or accreditation.

The sequence

Ten stages, from brief to support.

  1. Agree the goal and requirements

    Agree who will use the map, what they need it to do and how the work will be checked before design begins.

    OutputWritten requirements and project scope

  2. Review existing maps and records

    Check current plans, data and records for age, coverage and quality, then list what is missing.

    OutputData review and list of gaps

  3. Spatial data collection and preparation

    Collect field data where needed, georeference drawings, clean geometry and resolve conflicting sources.

    OutputPrepared source dataset

  4. Plan how the map and tools will work

    Agree how locations will be named, stored, shared and connected to other information.

    OutputSystem plan and data guide

  5. Build the maps and tools

    Digitise, build networks, construct the database, implement services and interfaces against the architecture.

    OutputWorking build in a test environment

  6. Connect information and set up access

    Connect the agreed sources, set up user permissions, load the data and resolve import errors.

    OutputConnected system

  7. Test the map and tools

    Check the data, permissions, accessibility and routes against the agreed requirements.

    OutputTest report and list of issues

  8. Deployment and handover

    Release to the agreed environment, confirm access, migrate or seed data and record the release.

    OutputDeployment record and acceptance sign-off

  9. Documentation and knowledge transfer

    Provide administrator and user documentation, run training on the client's own data and hand over ownership.

    OutputManuals, training record and handover pack

  10. Technical support and maintenance

    Agree the support arrangement: response route, review cadence, update procedure and scope of changes.

    OutputSupport agreement and maintenance plan

Data quality

Checks we run on spatial data

  • Geometry validity: self-intersections, slivers, duplicate vertices.
  • Topology: gaps, overlaps, dangling edges and unreachable nodes.
  • Attribute completeness against the schema's required fields.
  • Identifier uniqueness and referential integrity.
  • Coordinate reference consistency across layers.
  • Route sanity: every destination reachable from every documented origin.
  • Step-free continuity: no stair-only segment in an accessible route.

Acceptance

What sign-off means

Acceptance is against the criteria written at the start, not against a general impression at the end. A deliverable is accepted when the agreed tests pass, known limitations are documented and the client contact confirms the record.

  • Each criterion has a test and a result.
  • Open defects are listed with severity and an agreed action.
  • Limitations are stated in the handover pack.
  • Out-of-scope items are named, not implied.

Working with clients

What we need from the client side.

  • A named project contact with authority to approve deliverables.
  • Access to the plans, lists and systems that already exist.
  • Site access or a nominated person who can verify conditions.
  • A decision on hosting, data ownership and access before build starts.
  • Feedback within agreed review windows.

Where these are not available, the scope note records the assumption and the effect on the deliverable. It is not left to be discovered during handover.

Questions

Common questions about delivery.

How long does a project take?

An assessment can take one to three weeks. A mapping package typically runs four to twelve weeks depending on levels and field verification. A platform build is scoped separately and usually runs longer.

Can you work with our existing supplier?

Yes, where roles are clear. We can take the spatial data and engineering scope while another supplier handles infrastructure, or provide review and QA over their work.

What happens if the source drawings are wrong?

The gap is recorded and a field check is quoted. We do not silently correct geometry and present it as drawn from the plans.

Do you sign NDAs?

Yes, where the client requires one. Confidential material is kept out of public case studies unless written permission is given.
