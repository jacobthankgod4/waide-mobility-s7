What it is

A map, a route and a record.

Most products do one of these. Waide runs all three on the same map, so what a visitor does becomes the data an operator works from.

The map

A digital twin of the building

Every public floor, unit, shelf, lift and parking bay, drawn from the plans you already have.

  • Multi-level, including parking
  • Tenant names, entrances and hours
  • Updated when the venue changes
The route

Directions a visitor can follow

A QR code at the entrance opens the map in the phone browser. No app, no account.

  • Search by product, store, counter or exit
  • Step-free routes for wheelchairs and pushchairs
  • Local African languages
The record

What happened on the floor

Searches, routes, drop-off points and zone traffic, reported to the operator every week.

  • What people looked for
  • Where they went and where they stopped
  • Which zones underperform

Capabilities

What is in every deployment

These are part of the platform, not add-ons. Pricing covers the map, the navigation and the dashboard; integrations and hardware are quoted separately if you need them.

Product-level search

Search for a product and land at the shelf, not just the store. At Novare Lekki that covers 900 Shoprite shelves.

Step-free routing

Lifts and step-free paths are marked, so a wheelchair user or a parent with a pushchair gets a route that works.

QR entry

A code at each entrance opens the map in the browser. Nothing to install, which matters where phone storage and data are limited.

Local languages

Navigation and labels can run in local African languages as well as English.

Weekly reporting

Search demand, zone traffic, corridor flow and drop-off, sent to the venue team every week.

Multi-level mapping

Ground floor, upper levels and parking are part of the same route, not separate maps.

Deployment

How a venue goes live

  1. Floor plans and scope

    Send the plans and a tenant list. We confirm the levels, destinations and modules in a one-page scope note.

  2. Mapping

    We build the twin, index stores and shelves, and check routes on site. A single venue usually takes a few weeks; older or larger sites take longer.

  3. Launch and reporting

    QR points go up, the web app goes live, and the first weekly report follows. A 90–180 day pilot ends with a written evaluation.

Questions

Common questions

Do visitors need to download an app?

No. A QR code at the entrance opens the map in the phone browser. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

Does it need beacons or other hardware?

No. Navigation runs from the QR scan and the phone browser. Beacons and kiosks are not part of a standard deployment; if a venue wants them, they are quoted separately.

Can visitors search for a product, not just a store?

Yes, where the venue gives us the product data. At Novare Lekki, 900 Shoprite shelves are indexed, so a search can end at the aisle and shelf.

Who owns the data?

The venue owns the data collected in its building. We remove personal identifiers, never sell personal data, and set retention terms in the contract.

Does it connect to our POS or ERP?

Not today. Reports are delivered from Waide and can be exported. If an integration matters to you, tell us during scoping and we will say whether it is possible.

How long does mapping take?

A single venue usually takes a few weeks from receiving usable plans. Multi-level or older buildings take longer, and the scope note gives you a date before you commit.
