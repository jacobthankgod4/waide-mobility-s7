Outdoor to indoor

The last 100 metres are the hard part.

A street map stops at the door. Inside, the route has to deal with levels, lifts, closed corridors and a building that was never designed to explain itself.

Where it breaks

Three places people lose the trail.

01

The car park

Street navigation ends at a pin. The driver still has to find the lift, the right level and the mall entrance.

02

The entrance

Inside, the phone's blue dot drifts or disappears. The visitor is standing in a lobby with no idea which direction is correct.

03

The level change

The destination is on Level 1. The route has to say which lift, which side, and whether there is a step-free option.

What we do

A link or a QR code takes over.

The outdoor leg is handled by whatever the visitor already uses. Waide handles the moment they arrive, and everything after it.

Before arrival

  • A link can sit in a booking confirmation, a website or a message from the venue
  • The link opens the building map in the browser
  • No app install, no account, no location permission to start

On arrival

  • A QR code at each entrance opens the same map
  • The visitor picks a destination and gets a route from that entrance
  • Parking, lifts and facilities are part of the same map

The handoff is deliberate, not automatic. The visitor scans or taps, and the indoor route starts. We do not claim a seamless handover from Google Maps or Apple Maps.

Limits

What we do not do today.

Not offered Why it matters
Automatic handoff from a street navigation app There is no integration with Google Maps or Apple Maps. The handoff is a tap or a scan.
Live indoor positioning The map does not track the visitor's exact position. It shows the route from a chosen start point.
Beacon or Wi-Fi positioning No hardware is installed, and none is required for the route to work.
Outdoor routing between sites We map the building and its immediate approaches. Street-level routing stays with the tools people already use.

Measurement

How the handoff is judged.

QR scan to route

Share of entrance scans that start a route in the same session. A low number means the entry point or the first screen is failing.

Drop-off zone

Where routes are abandoned. In a multi-level building, this often shows up at the lift lobby or the second entrance.

Both are defined in the pilot scope before launch, alongside the other four metrics on the platform page.

Questions

Common questions

Can the map start from the car park?

Yes, if the parking level is in scope. Parking Level A is mapped at Novare Lekki, and the route starts from the bay or the lift lobby.

Does it need GPS indoors?

No. The map does not rely on GPS inside the building. The visitor picks a start point, usually the entrance they used, and follows the route.

Can we put the link in our booking emails?

Yes. A link to the venue map can go wherever the venue already communicates with visitors. It opens in the browser like any other link.

Why not just use a street navigation app?

Because street maps do not know about units, levels, lifts or a corridor that closes on Mondays. The indoor map does.

Is this available outside malls?

The same handoff works for hospitals, campuses, stations and offices. The live reference is still the Novare Lekki mall pilot.

Map the walk from the door.

Send a plan and tell us where visitors arrive from. We will show you the route from that point to the destinations that matter.
