How we mapped Novare Lekki Mall

We mapped three levels of the mall in Lekki, from the car park to the shops. The map includes 150+ store locations, over 1,000 parking bays and 900 Shoprite shelf locations, with separate walking and vehicle routes.

01 · Project overview

What we mapped

We used the mall's floor plans and site observations to map shops, corridors, facilities, parking bays and supermarket shelves. The mapped places are connected so a route planner can use them.

We aligned the floor plans with the map, traced the features, kept walking routes separate from vehicle routes and checked sample routes on site.

About these figures: 22,000 m² is the reported mall area covered by the project. Store, parking-bay and shelf numbers are map counts, not sales or visitor figures.

02 · Location

Lekki, Lagos, Nigeria.

Novare Lekki Mall is a multi-level shopping centre with a supermarket, more than 150 stores and a large car park.

A visitor arriving by car must find a parking space, enter the building, move between floors and locate a shop or shelf. The map connects these parts of the trip.

03 · Context

Indoor maps have to match each building

Each building uses its own floor names, room labels and access rules. These details need to be checked against the plans and the building itself.

Shops move and layouts change. A map that is accurate on launch day can become wrong when a tenant moves, a wall changes or a walkway closes. The map needs a way to be updated.

  • Floor plans may not line up with real-world locations.
  • Floor names can differ between plans, signs and shop lists.
  • Service corridors and back-of-house areas are not on public plans.
  • Parking geometry has to be captured separately from the retail floors.
  • Accessible routes depend on lift status and temporary obstructions.

04 · Route planning

Separate routes for cars and pedestrians

Cars and pedestrians need different routes. A car route ends at a parking bay; the walking route starts there and continues to a shop. We mapped the two separately and marked where they meet.

Finding a shop is useful; finding a product requires more detail. We mapped 900 Shoprite shelf locations, with more detail than elsewhere in the mall.

Car and walking routes

We mapped vehicle and walking routes separately and marked where visitors change from one to the other.

Links between floors

Lifts, stairs, escalators and ramps connect the mapped floors, with access details recorded where known.

08 · Tools and technologies

Open tools, chosen for control.

  • JOSM for indoor digitisation, tagging and geometry editing.
  • OpenStreetMap-style indoor tagging for levels, units, corridors, doors and facilities.
  • Georeferencing against available imagery and control points.
  • Graph construction for separate pedestrian and vehicle networks.
  • Dijkstra shortest-path routing with mode-specific edge costs.
  • Browser-based delivery for visitor access without an app install.

No proprietary computer vision or generative model is credited with the digitised geometry. The parking bays, units and routes were drawn and checked by hand.

09 · Mapping and digitisation workflow

Draw, tag, connect, check.

A floor plan shows rooms and walls, but not how people can move between them. We mapped which corridors connect to lifts, which doors are public and which routes avoid stairs.

  1. Trace the building shell and floor boundary.
  2. Draw units and assign tenant and category attributes.
  3. Draw circulation space and connect it to entrances and lifts.
  4. Add facilities and accessible points of interest.
  5. Build the pedestrian graph and a separate step-free graph.
  6. Build the vehicle graph across Parking Level A.
  7. Index shelves inside the anchor supermarket.
  8. Verify on site and correct the topology.

Quality rules applied

  • No orphaned corridors or unreachable units.
  • Every level change uses a named connector.
  • Vehicle and pedestrian edges never share a node without a transfer point.
  • Every store has a stable identifier.
  • Step-free edges are tagged and validated, not inferred.

10 · Vehicle and pedestrian routing

Two graphs, one journey.

The map links the parking area to walking routes through the mall, with a marked point where visitors leave the car park and continue on foot.

Vehicle routing uses the parking layout: aisles, bay access and exit points. Pedestrian routing uses the retail floor: corridors, doors, lifts, escalators and stairs. A transfer node connects a parking bay to the nearest pedestrian entrance or lift lobby.

Dijkstra's algorithm computes the shortest path over the relevant graph. Edge costs differ by mode: a vehicle edge is not weighted like a walking edge, and a step-free route may accept a longer distance to avoid a stair.

13 · Validation and quality control

Checked against the building.

  • Geometry reviewed for gaps, overlaps and unreachable areas.
  • Every level connector checked against the physical lift or stair.
  • Routes walked end to end, including at least one step-free alternative.
  • Entrances and closures confirmed with the venue team.
  • Vehicle routes checked against the parking layout and one-way flows.
  • Findings logged and corrected before the route layer was accepted.
