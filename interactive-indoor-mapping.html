Indoor mapping

A map of the building, kept current.

The map is the asset. Navigation, reporting and leasing conversations run on the same drawing, so nobody is working from a different version of the floor.

What it holds

Everything a route needs to know.

A floor plan is a picture. A map has structure: what each space is, how it connects, and which paths a person can walk.

Spaces

Units and rooms

Store footprints, departments, wards, gates, bays and meeting rooms, each with a name a visitor would use.

Connections

Lifts, stairs and doors

Level changes are drawn as connections, so a route knows whether it can use them and whether they are step-free.

Paths

Walkable routes

The map records the paths people can take, not the straight line between two points on a plan.

Context

Facilities and hours

Toilets, parking, prayer rooms, first aid, service desks and opening hours, attached to the right space.

Content

Store and product names

Brand names, categories, synonyms and, where supplied, product names that point to an aisle and shelf.

Change

A record of edits

When a unit changes hands or a corridor closes, the map is updated and the old route is no longer offered.

How it is built

Plans, a walk, then a test.

  1. Start from what you have

    Floor plans in PDF, CAD or image format, plus a tenant or department list. If the plan is out of date, we survey the space first.

  2. Draw and index

    We trace the levels, units, facilities and connections, then attach names, hours and product data where it exists.

  3. Walk it on site

    Someone walks the routes with the map open. Wrong doors, closed corridors and missing lifts are corrected before launch.

  4. Keep it current

    Directory and tenancy changes are applied as they happen. A physical renovation is re-mapped as a separate, quoted job.

Questions

Common questions

How accurate is the map?

Routes are walked on site before launch. Accuracy depends on the plans and the walkthrough; where the plan is wrong, the survey corrects it. We do not publish a centimetre accuracy figure.

Can you map a building without digital plans?

Yes, through a measured survey. That is a separate quoted piece of work, and it is included in the scope note before you commit.

Who owns the map?

The venue owns the data about its building. The mapping tools and platform remain Waide's. Contract terms cover use, export and what happens at the end of an agreement.

What happens when a store changes hands?

The venue sends the change and the map, directory and search index are updated. Directory changes are included for the term of the agreement.

Can we use the map in our own app?

Not today. There is no public API or embeddable map. The visitor experience is hosted by Waide and opened by QR or link.

We will tell you what can be mapped from it, what needs a survey, and what the first version would cover.
