Not offered

We do not sell ads on the map.

Proximity advertising needs beacons, an ad server and a consent model we do not run. This page explains what it would take, and what the map does instead.

The model

What proximity advertising usually means.

The common version is simple to describe and hard to do honestly: detect a phone near a store, push an offer to it, and charge someone for the impression.

Detection

Beacons or location data identify that a phone is near a specific point. That requires hardware and a permission the visitor must grant.

Delivery

An ad server decides which message to show, to whom, and records the impression. This is a separate system from a map.

Consent

Location-based marketing needs a lawful basis, a clear notice and a way to opt out. In many markets it is a regulatory question before it is a product question.

Waide does none of these three things today. No beacons are installed, no ad inventory is sold, and no location data is collected outside the building.

What the map does

Venue notices, set by the venue.

The map can carry information the operator already controls. That is not advertising, and it is not targeted at a person.

On the map

  • Opening hours and temporary closures
  • Store or department names and locations
  • Facilities such as toilets, first aid and prayer rooms
  • Directions to a service desk or exit

Not on the map

  • Paid placements or sponsored stores
  • Offers pushed because of where someone is standing
  • Messages sold to a third-party advertiser
  • Profiles built across visits

If you need it

What an advertising build would require.

  1. Hardware

    Beacons or another positioning method, installed and maintained. This is a separate capital and operating cost.

  2. A consent and privacy model

    A lawful basis, a notice, an opt-out and a retention policy. It has to survive a procurement review, not just a demo.

  3. An advertising partner

    An ad server and a sales team, or a partner who brings both. Waide does not have either today.

If a venue wants to explore this, we will say plainly that it is not in the product and introduce the conversation as a separate project with its own scope and risks.

Questions

Common questions

Do you use beacons at all?

No. The standard deployment uses QR codes and the phone browser. Nothing is installed in the ceiling.

Can a store pay to appear first in search?

No. Search ranking is not for sale. Results are ordered by relevance to the query and the visitor's chosen start point.

Can we send a notice about a closure?

The map can show closures and hours as part of the venue content. Send the change to your Waide contact and it is applied to the map.

Do you collect location data for marketing?

No. Waide does not collect precise location outside the venue and does not build profiles across visits.

Will this change?

It could, but not without notice. If advertising is ever added, it will be a separate, opt-in product with its own terms, and this page will say so before it launches.

Ask what the map can show.

If you need a notice, a closure or a service message on the map, that is a content question. If you need an ad product, we will tell you it is not us.
