Scope

Included and not included.

Included

  • Digital twin of every public level in scope
  • QR entry points, short links and the visitor web app
  • Turn-by-turn wayfinding to stores, shelves, counters, lifts and toilets
  • Operator dashboard: zone traffic, search demand, drop-off, route completion
  • Directory and tenant changes during the term
  • Accessible routes shown on the map
  • Email support with a named contact
  • Quarterly performance review

Not included

  • Hardware such as beacons or kiosks, if the venue needs them
  • Custom integrations with ERP, POS or booking systems
  • On-site staff or a managed help desk
  • Advertising sales or media representation
  • Translation into additional languages
  • Third-party accessibility certification or audit
  • Re-mapping after renovation or reconfiguration
  • Service levels beyond the standard support terms

Method

How a quote is built.

No two venues map the same way. Four things set the price, and we show you each one before you sign.

  1. 01

    Floor area and levels

    Total public area and the number of levels set the mapping effort. Back-of-house and service corridors are excluded unless you ask for them.

  2. 02

    Destinations

    Stores, shelves, counters, departments and facilities that a visitor can search for. A 900-shelf supermarket takes longer than a 40-store mall.

  3. 03

    Modules

    Navigation and the operator dashboard are standard. Accessibility routing, multi-language and integrations are priced separately.

  4. 04

    Support level

    Standard email support is included. Priority response, on-site training and quarterly business reviews are available on larger agreements.

Before you commit

What happens in the first 30 days.

Scoping is a short fixed-price piece of work. It ends with a written scope, a fixed quote and the measures the pilot will be judged on.

  1. Week 1 — Floor-plan review. We check what you have, what is missing, and whether the space is a fit.
  2. Week 2 — Scope note. Levels, destinations, modules and exclusions, in one page.
  3. Week 3 — Fixed quote. Pilot fee, rollout fee and the assumptions behind both.
  4. Week 4 — Success measures. The metrics the pilot will be judged on, agreed in writing.
