The problem

Building information is often hard to find.

Plans, room lists, signs and staff knowledge are often kept separately. Visitors may need to ask for directions, while facility teams can struggle to find current information about rooms, routes and equipment.

We bring these details together in maps and digital tools. Visitors can use them to find places and step-free routes; facility teams can use them to locate rooms and equipment.

Information kept in separate places

Floor plans, shop lists and equipment records are often stored in different files and systems, making them hard to compare or update.

Navigation that stops at the door

Street maps do not know about units, levels, lifts or a corridor that closes on Mondays. Visitors ask staff instead.

Hard-to-find rooms and equipment

When building records are spread across different systems, staff can have trouble finding the room or equipment information they need.

Accessibility added late

Step-free routes and screen-reader support are treated as an upgrade, after the map and the interface are already fixed.

Delivery

A clear plan before work begins.

We agree on the work, review each stage with you and confirm the next steps.

Our approach

  1. Discover and assess

    Agree what the map or tool needs to do, then review the plans and records you already have.

    OutputProject plan and list of missing information

  2. Collect and structure spatial data

    Align floor plans to the site, map rooms and routes, and organize the related details.

    OutputChecked map data

  3. Engineer and integrate

    Build the map, route planner and any agreed connections to other systems.

    OutputWorking test version

  4. Validate and deploy

    Walk the routes, test the map and check that it meets the agreed requirements.

    OutputTest results and launch approval

  5. Train, document and support

    Hand over the documentation, train the team that will run the system and agree the support arrangement.

    OutputManuals, training record and support plan

Accessible routes

Plan accessible routes from the start.

Step-free routes can help wheelchair users, people with pushchairs, visitors carrying bags and staff moving equipment. We map these routes so visitors can choose a path that meets their needs.

  • Step-free routes built from lifts, ramps and level access, checked on site.
  • Maps that work with screen readers, with audio directions where needed.
  • Important places marked on the map, including entrances, toilets and help desks.
  • Accessibility testing with disabled users where the project allows it.
