Procurement automation

The checks a request passes before a quotation goes out.

Requests for quotation, tenders and prequalification arrive at a monitored inbox. This page describes what the software does with one, what it refuses to decide, and what it will never send on its own.

The software prepares, extracts, screens and checks. A named person decides. Where a mandatory fact is unknown, the answer is a hold rather than an assumption.

A single request, end to end

From an e-mail to a recorded decision.

The same sequence runs for a two-line consumables RFQ and for a multi-lot tender. Nothing leaves the process without the step before it having produced a result.

  1. Intake

    The request arrives by e-mail at a monitored inbox. The sender, subject and received time are recorded before anything is opened, and attachments are stored with their original bytes.

    ResultA recorded request with its documents attached and its timestamp fixed

  2. Extraction

    Text is pulled from each document page by page, including scanned pages read by OCR. The extraction records what it could not read instead of filling the gap.

    ResultRequirement text with unreadable pages listed explicitly

  3. Requirements

    Mandatory requirements, submission rules, deadlines, delivery terms and evaluation criteria are separated from background narrative and put into a checklist.

    ResultA requirements list that can be checked line by line

  4. Screening

    Suppliers and every other transaction party are screened against the OFAC SDN and UN consolidated lists. A name match is recorded as a potential match until a named person resolves it with reasons.

    ResultA screening result, with false positives identified as such

  5. Bid gate

    Sixteen deterministic checks cover legal identity, mandatory evidence, supplier approval, sanctions, product regulatory status, export control, the jurisdictional chain, contribution margin, cost completeness, peak cash, liability, amendments, deadline, evidence validity and signatory.

    ResultBid-ready, hold, or no-bid, with the blocking reason named

  6. Authority

    The decision is routed to the role that holds it. A second, different authority is required above the threshold, the same person cannot satisfy both, and the approval binds to the exact figures it authorised and expires after 72 hours.

    ResultA signed decision, or a recorded refusal

A changed amount, quantity or beneficiary voids an approval rather than inheriting it. A submission without an official receipt leaves the request at “approved” and does not proceed.

Controls

What the software enforces, and what happens when it cannot.

These are rules in code rather than instructions in a prompt. A failure is a refusal and an unknown is a hold, in both cases recorded.

ControlWhat is enforcedWhen a fact is unknown
Authority matrix 25 named capabilities across six roles. The software holds no signature, approval, payment-release or guarantee authority at all. Refused; the role that holds the right is asked to decide.
Dual control Above the threshold a second, different authority must decide. The same person cannot give both decisions. Held at the first decision.
Bid gate Sixteen checks, each recorded with its own result and blocking reason. Hold. Never a pass.
Sanctions screening OFAC SDN and UN consolidated lists, refreshed with the source URL and hash of every file used. A potential match blocks the bid until a named person resolves it.
Evidence Source, issuing entity, issue and expiry dates, access class and permitted use per document. Conflicts between documents are flagged. Expired or conflicting evidence blocks the bid.
Inbound correspondence 20 intent classes. External text is handled as data, never as an instruction. Bank-detail changes and requests for confidential documents are quarantined. Quarantined for human review.
Payments Three-way match between purchase order, delivery and invoice. Preparing, approving and releasing are separate roles. No preparation without a match.
Record Append-only ledger, each event hashed with the one before it. Rewriting any event breaks verification. A broken chain is reported as an integrity failure.

Disclosure

What this page does not show, and why.

Not published

  • Supplier names, contacts and bank details
  • Buyer names and tender documents
  • Prices, margins and cash positions
  • Correspondence and approval records
  • Which opportunities are being pursued

Why

Those records belong to the parties in a live procurement, and publishing them would damage a buyer’s position or a supplier’s confidence. The status above is limited to the currency of public reference data and the coverage of controls that exist in code.

Where a buyer or supplier needs evidence for their own file, it is issued for a named opportunity rather than posted here.

Where it stops

Things the process will not do, even when asked.

No invented credentials. A certificate, licence or approval exists only if a document in the evidence file proves it and has not expired. Fictitious references and unevidenced claims are refused rather than softened.

No bid without evidence. A missing mandatory requirement produces a hold, not a best guess, and a deadline that has passed cannot be reopened by an approval.

No payment without a match. An invoice that does not match its purchase order and its delivery cannot be prepared, and a changed beneficiary voids the release rather than inheriting it.

No instruction from a document. Text inside an attachment or an e-mail that tells the system to pay, send or change a bank account is treated as content to report, never as a command to follow.

This is a control description, not a certification. It is not an ISO claim, an audit opinion, or an approved-supplier status.
