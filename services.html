How work starts

Four ways to work with us.

Before work begins, we agree on what will be delivered, how it will be checked and what is outside the project.

Assessment

Data and feasibility review

A short review of existing plans and records, with a list of missing information and a recommended next step.

Mapping

Indoor map build

Map conversion, place names, walking routes and checks, delivered as a documented dataset.

Platform

Geospatial application

Database, API and interface built around one operational decision, deployed and handed over with documentation.

Advisory

Technical advice

Requirements, specifications, tender support and checks alongside your team or supplier.

Where it applies

Buildings and infrastructure where spatial information has to work.

Retail and mixed-use

Malls, markets and large stores: tenant mapping, shelf-level search, parking networks and visitor routing.

Healthcare

Hospitals and clinics: department wayfinding, step-free routes, accessible facilities and front-desk load.

Transport

Airports, stations and interchanges: multi-level routing, gates, platforms, facilities and accessible connections.

Education and public

Campuses, libraries and civic buildings: building directories, room finding and inclusive access information.

Workplace and estates

Offices, mixed estates and industrial sites: visitor routing, space inventories and asset visibility.

Infrastructure programmes

GIS data structures, QA frameworks and advisory support for programmes that need a spatial evidence base.

Questions

Before you commission a service.

Can you work from drawings only?

Often, yes. If the drawings are out of date or incomplete, a measured survey or field check is added as a separate, quoted task.

Do you provide the software as well as the data?

Where the scope includes a platform, yes: database, interface and documentation. Where it does not, we deliver the dataset and specification so another team can build on it.

How do projects start?

With a short scoping conversation, then a written scope note covering deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, acceptance criteria and cost.

Next step

Tell us what the system has to decide.

A venue type, a country and one operational question is enough to start. We will tell you which service division fits and what the first stage would cover.
