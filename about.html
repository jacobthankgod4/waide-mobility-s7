About the company

A spatial systems consultancy, built around the work.

Waide Mobility exists to make buildings and infrastructure legible to the people who use and operate them. We do that through geospatial engineering, structured digital models and interfaces that hold up in daily use.

Purpose

The physical world is under-described.

A large building can be surveyed, leased, maintained and operated for decades without ever having a single, reliable description of its internal space. The knowledge sits in drawings, in spreadsheets and in the heads of long-serving staff.

That gap has practical consequences. Visitors cannot find a department. A facilities team cannot locate an asset. A planner cannot compare sites. An accessibility audit cannot see the routes people actually take.

Waide's purpose is to close that gap with structured spatial information: geometry that is connected, attributes that are queryable and a model that someone can maintain after we leave.

Focus

Three disciplines, one delivery.

01

Geospatial engineering

Turning drawings, imagery and field observations into validated spatial data, route networks and spatial databases.

02

Digital twins

Structuring building and asset information so that spaces, systems and records can be queried together.

03

Digital infrastructure

Building the platforms, APIs and interfaces that make spatial data usable, and the documentation that keeps them running.

Software engineering, automation and computer vision are enabling capabilities in this work. They are not the company's primary identity, and we do not present them as the service itself.

Approach

Small, senior and accountable.

Waide is deliberately small. The person who scopes a project is involved in delivering it, and the technical record is written by the people who did the work.

That model has limits: we take on a narrow set of assignments and we say no when the scope does not fit. It also has advantages. There is no handover between a sales team and a delivery team, no junior analyst learning the venue at the client's expense, and no distance between the data and the decision.

Inclusion

Accessibility is a design constraint, not a feature.

A spatial system that only works for a confident, sighted, mobile user has not solved the problem for the venue. It has solved it for the majority and left everyone else to ask staff.

We model step-free routes as a separate network, attach access conditions to facilities, and build text-first interfaces alongside map views. Where a project allows, we test with disabled users and record what changed as a result.

Technology and research interests

Where we are investing time.

  • Indoor data models that survive tenant churn and refits.
  • Accessible routing that treats level changes as first-class data.
  • Spatial data quality checks that can run without a specialist.
  • Low-bandwidth, browser-first interfaces for public venues.
  • BIM and GIS integration where the source models support it.
  • Open formats and exit routes that avoid platform lock-in.

Operating identity

What we are and are not.

  • An independent consultancy based in Lagos, Nigeria.
  • A specialist in geospatial and digital systems for the built environment.
  • Not a general-purpose software agency or a SaaS company.
  • Not a recruitment or personal CV site.
  • Not an approved supplier or certified body unless separately evidenced.
  • Not a company that publishes client work without permission.

Vision

Spatial intelligence as ordinary infrastructure.

The long-term goal is not a single flagship deployment. It is a working norm in which buildings and infrastructure come with usable spatial information as a matter of course, in the same way they come with electrical plans and fire exits.

That means data models that can be handed over, methods that can be taught, and systems that do not depend on one supplier to stay correct. It also means accessibility information being part of the base dataset rather than a separate project.

Waide is early in that work. The Novare Lekki pilot is a first reference, not a finished portfolio. We would rather state that plainly than imply a scale we have not reached.
